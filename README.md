### Open Framework and Proprietary Implementations

The architecture is intended to define a **free and broadly usable foundational framework** for authorization-gated AI agents. Its purpose is to establish common concepts, interfaces, roles, invariants, and architectural principles that can be implemented and extended by different parties.

“Free” in this context refers to the intended accessibility and usability of the architectural framework. The precise legal conditions of reuse, modification, implementation, and redistribution are determined by the license under which the framework is published.

The framework does not require implementations of the architecture to be open source or to disclose their internal technology.

In particular, advanced implementation concepts—such as highly efficient Security Cores, specialized Decisioners, optimized enforcement mechanisms, hardware-assisted security components, verification methods, or particularly effective resource-management techniques—may be developed independently and, where legally appropriate, protected as intellectual property.

The distinction is intentional:

> **The framework should remain broadly accessible; innovation in its implementation should remain economically and technically incentivizable.**

A common architectural foundation can therefore encourage interoperability and independent development without requiring every technological improvement built upon that foundation to be publicly disclosed.

The framework may be published under a license that explicitly permits broad implementation and extension while preserving any rights that the authors intend to retain. The choice of license is therefore a separate governance decision and should not be confused with the architectural definition itself.

The architecture makes no claim that a particular implementation technique qualifies for patent, copyright, trade-secret, or another form of legal protection. Such questions remain subject to applicable law and the specific characteristics of the implementation.
